# SPHEREx inverse imaging requires calibrated evidence

SPHEREx DR1/QR3 supports a defensible experiment in native-pixel source modeling, but its calibration products do not establish that NADIR can resolve new companions or reconstruct finer astronomical structure. The appropriate first comparison fits one and two sources to identical measurements, challenges the result with plausible single-source alternatives, and calibrates improvement through simulations that repeat the search. Multiple exposures can improve localization through noise averaging; demonstrating additional resolving power requires a separate comparison at matched information budgets. Instrument artifacts, uncertain registration, spectral differences and correlated noise can invalidate otherwise persuasive fits. This report defines physical and statistical assumptions from literature and archive documentation; **it contains no NADIR implementation benchmark or confirmed astronomical detection**.

## Native pixels preserve the instrument model

The archive ePSF already includes pixel response. Its fivefold oversampling describes response at different subpixel phases; it does not increase telescope resolution fivefold. Sample it at native pixel centers, using the correct detector zone and image orientation. Integrating it over pixels again would apply pixel response twice. The official tutorial also normalizes rendered stamps at their actual pixel phase, motivating a flux-conservation check before changing NADIR's existing normalization. ([Official ePSF tutorial](https://caltech-ipac.github.io/irsa-tutorials/spherex-psf/))

DR1/QR3 ePSFs are 33×33 arrays. Detector 3 uses 11×41 zones; the other detectors use 21×21. IMAGE carries MJy/sr surface brightness, includes zodiacal emission, and has an accompanying variance image in squared units. **WCS-WAVE is intended for visualization; science analysis requires CWAVE and CBAND calibration maps.** Consequently, spectral temperatures derived only from cutout wavelength lookups need an explicit approximation label. ([Archive product specification](https://caltech-ipac.github.io/spherex-archive-documentation/spherex-data-products/))

NADIR should preserve exposure identity, calibration version, masks, coordinates and provenance throughout every fit. Repeated records must not count as independent observations. The proposed experimental workflow should apply stricter contamination checks without silently changing the established motion estimator. Official forced photometry rejects several detector-quality and artifact flags beyond saturation and can jointly fit specified companions. Those catalog-constrained fits provide a useful comparison for free source decomposition. ([IRSA spectrophotometry](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/onlinehelp/spherex/spherex/sp.html))

## A second fitted component needs calibrated alternatives

At zero secondary flux, the secondary position becomes unidentified; at zero separation, components become degenerate. The ordinary chi-square likelihood-ratio reference therefore cannot be assumed. Astronomical component testing has documented this failure at parameter boundaries. ([Protassov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0201547)) Ordinary BIC also loses its usual marginal-likelihood interpretation for singular mixture models. Report its penalty as a descriptive comparison, not model odds. ([Drton and Plummer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.0911))

A practical proposed procedure fits H1, simulates its images using specified PSF and noise assumptions, then refits H1 and H2 with identical masks, parameter bounds and multistarts. For B simulations, report `(1 + exceedances)/(B + 1)`, the seed, B and simulation resolution. **This is conditional plug-in evidence, not the probability that a companion exists.** The plus-one convention avoids zero Monte Carlo tails; fitting nuisance parameters and misspecifying the PSF remain separate limitations. ([Phipson and Smyth](https://gksmyth.github.io/pubs/PermPValuesPreprint.pdf))

NADIR-specific decision gates should require acceptable residuals, optimizer convergence, no influential masked core, and stability against broadened or asymmetric single-source models. Separation and orientation should agree in independent exposures after coordinate transformation. These are proposed safeguards, not universal numerical thresholds established by the literature. Uncertainty should remain unavailable when degeneracies defeat estimation. Moreover, zero false splits in 20 independent trials still permits a one-sided 95% binomial upper rate of about 14%, calculated as `1 − 0.05^(1/20)`.

## Dither gains must survive registration and noise

Drizzle demonstrates how weighted linear reconstruction can combine undersampled, dithered observations while accounting for distortion. It establishes a useful baseline method, not SPHEREx-specific performance. ([Fruchter and Hook](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9808087)) Redistributing an input measurement among output pixels creates correlated errors. Output variance must propagate squared weights, and a diagonal variance image cannot represent the full covariance needed for aperture or model-fit errors. ([STScI correlated-noise documentation](https://hst-docs.stsci.edu/drizzpac/chapter-3-description-of-the-drizzle-algorithm/3-3-weight-maps-and-correlated-noise))

The proposed experiment should compare single-frame fitting, native-grid coaddition and joint native-frame fitting, then contrast identical phases with diverse dithers at matched exposure counts and noise budgets. Measure source-position error, flux bias, false splits and prediction of held-out native pixels. Registration jitter and PSF mismatch belong in the injected failures. A sharper display or better centroid from repeated measurements alone does not demonstrate super-resolution.

SPHEREx instrumentation includes interpixel capacitance and spatially correlated electronic noise mechanisms. Delivered pixel variances therefore do not prove independent Gaussian errors. ([Instrument performance paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29835v1)) No release-specific covariance kernel or applicable registration floor was established in these notes; real cutout metadata and isolated-source residuals must supply the relevant tests.

## Spectral and anomaly inference require independent validation

Spectra combine epochs. IRSA identifies saturation-related spectral structure near Jansky brightness and warns that forced positions wrong by roughly 1.5–2 arcseconds can substantially lose signal. Variability and crowding therefore confound a static thermal interpretation. ([IRSA FAQ](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/onlinehelp/spherex/spherex/faq.html)) A blend whose component ratio varies can also shift its centroid without physical motion; this is a direct forward-model consequence to quantify through injections.

An anomaly framework should retain definitions, units, uncertainties, provenance and missing reasons. Valid marginal null tails can support a conservative multiplicity-adjusted screen; uncalibrated inputs support only heuristic ranking. Outlier algorithms identify unusual samples relative to their fitted population, and their thresholds do not establish astrophysical novelty. ([scikit-learn outlier documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/outlier_detection.html)) All epochs of one source must remain in the same validation group, with additional held-out fields testing transfer. ([Grouped validation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html#cross-validation-iterators-for-grouped-data))

The defensible contribution is a reproducible test of where inverse modeling succeeds and where it fails. Real-sky discovery claims require independently vetted labels and follow-up evidence; synthetic recovery establishes only the tested operating conditions. The next decision should follow a cache and label census, then paired injection experiments that expose calibration sensitivity.
